# Empirical shortlist for ₹300,000 long-only NSE cash equity

Research date: 2026-09-07. Research only; no strategy code, backtests, broker access or orders. Five original research works below. Index-provider studies are first-party evidence about their indices, with a commercial incentive to present them favourably; they are not independent verification.

**First candidate: a liquid, diversified, medium-term cross-sectional momentum portfolio with low turnover.** This has the strongest combination of Indian evidence, an observable long-only implementation, and compatibility with the available price history. It is a candidate for validation, not a demonstrated profitable replacement. Evidence is strongest for months-long ownership. A compulsory days-to-few-weeks exit materially changes the hypothesis and loses that support.

The reported losses of 23–56% in four local variants over January 2025–September 2026 are supplied task context, not independently recomputed here. They reject those implementations on that sample; they do not establish that every momentum design fails. Available raw accounting records from January 2024 do not establish what fundamentals or earnings news were publicly known on each historical decision date.

## Decision and practical consequences

The following are research judgments, not additional empirical results:

1. **Test medium-term cross-sectional momentum first.** Start with the simplest price-only, liquidity-screened design and an explicit slow-turnover reference. Rank stocks against one another over approximately six/twelve months; allow holdings to survive for months. Freeze the universe eligibility, treatment of the latest month, corporate-action adjustments, weighting, review frequency and rank buffers before looking at outcomes. A monthly review with a retention buffer is a reasonable *separate adaptation* to study, not an improvement established by these sources. Avoid adding several timing indicators as a new source of degrees of freedom.
2. **Quality plus momentum is a later challenger.** Its rationale merits examination, but the reviewed evidence cannot show that quality rescues this strategy. Obtain historical financial statement versions and publication timestamps first; compare with identical pure-momentum universe, costs and sizing to isolate the extra signal. Current financial ratios cannot be backdated.
3. **Defer short-horizon reversal.** Its Indian evidence is concentrated in the liquidity conditions least friendly to frequent executable trades. The observed disappearance at longer holds also means it cannot simply borrow the slower candidate's cost structure. A credible future test needs spread/impact estimates, next-session fills, circuit constraints and explicit news/gap handling.
4. **Defer earnings drift until timestamped data exist.** The economic hypothesis is better defined than generic news sentiment, but releases, first-seen times, earnings expectations and revisions must be reconstructible. Validate the positive-surprise long leg independently; an academic hedge return is insufficient.

At ₹300,000, 20 equal positions imply ₹15,000 each (arithmetic, not a recommended allocation). Smaller tickets do not remove spread, sell-side charges, adverse gaps or no-fill circuit days; many small positions can magnify fixed per-sale charges. Compare strategy returns after actual charges and conservative slippage with a matched broad-market total-return benchmark and a feasible passive alternative. Increasing trade frequency must earn its additional costs.

The current approximately 32-month raw history contains few independent medium-term formation/holding cycles. It cannot support a robust multi-regime claim. Separate data integrity from economic validation; do not pick a design on its ability to reverse the already-observed 2025–2026 losses. Long pre-launch index histories are stress scenarios, not out-of-sample validation. None of these sources establishes a reliable daily/monthly income target or a tolerable drawdown for this user.
